What Is REM Sleep?

REM (rapid eye movement) sleep is the stage when your eyes dart under closed lids, your brain looks almost awake, most vivid dreams happen and most of your muscles go limp. In adults it fills about 20 to 25% of the night, and it comes back once every sleep cycle, getting longer toward morning.31

REM sleep in 60 seconds

  • 60–90 minAfter you fall asleep, the first REM period arrives.2
  • 4–6REM periods a night, one in each sleep cycle of about 80 to 110 minutes.14
  • 10→60Minutes: the first REM period is short, and the last can run up to about an hour.1
  • 2nd halfMost REM sleep falls in the second half of the night. Deep sleep owns the first.2

What happens in your body during REM

From the outside you look still. Inside, REM is the busiest stage of sleep. It is sometimes called paradoxical sleep for exactly that reason: an active brain in a body that barely moves.

  • EYES

    They dart

    Quick, jerky movements under closed lids, which gave the stage its name. A gaze center in the brainstem drives them.1

  • MUSCLES

    They go limp

    Most skeletal muscles lose their tone (atonia). The eyes and the diaphragm keep working, so you still breathe.1

  • BRAIN

    Almost awake

    Brain activity looks close to waking, and brain metabolism can rise by up to 20%. Memory and emotion areas are active while you dream.14

  • HEART & BREATH

    Uneven

    Breathing turns irregular, and pulse and blood pressure rise and vary, unlike the steady rhythm of non-REM sleep.15

  • TEMPERATURE

    Unregulated

    The body does not regulate its temperature well in REM, which is one reason a cold room can mean less of it.4

  • DREAMS

    Most vivid

    Most vivid, story-like dreams happen in REM, though dreaming can happen in any stage of sleep.510

When REM happens, and how many times a night

A night is not one long dive. You loop through light sleep, deep sleep and REM, again and again. Pick a cycle and watch where REM sits.

Deep sleep leads the night. REM closes it. A short night trims the REM end.

Why REM matters: what it seems to do

Honest first line: science has not settled what REM is for. A StatPearls review calls the purpose of sleep, and REM in particular, not well understood.2 These are the leads with the most behind them.

  • MEMORY · STRONG LEAD

    Filing what you learned

    During REM the brain runs a fast rhythm mode that appears to help consolidate some kinds of memory, especially skills. Deep sleep seems to handle other kinds.6

  • EMOTION · LEADING HYPOTHESIS

    Taking the sting out

    In a 2011 study, a night of sleep softened the brain’s alarm response (the amygdala) to upsetting pictures seen the day before, and the change tracked a marker of REM sleep.1312

  • DEVELOPMENT · LIKELY

    Building a brain

    Researchers think REM supports brain development, which may be why newborns spend so much of their sleep in it.5

Hormones and brain cleaning: is that REM?

Two popular claims, checked against the research. In both cases the answer points mostly to deep sleep, the slow delta stage, not to REM.

Growth hormoneIts biggest pulse comes shortly after you fall asleep, in deep slow-wave sleep. By the REM-rich second half of the night it is no longer being released.14
CortisolRises toward morning, mostly on the body clock rather than because of any one sleep stage.14
Brain cleaningIn mice, waste cleared about twice as fast asleep, and the cleaning flow tracked slow delta waves.1516
More on the brain’s rinse cycle

The brain has no lymph vessels like the rest of the body. A 2013 study in mice found that during sleep the space between brain cells widened and fluid washed through faster, clearing beta-amyloid, a protein linked with Alzheimer’s disease, more quickly than when the mice were awake.15

A 2019 follow-up found that this inflow rose with slow delta activity and fell as heart rate went up, which fits deep non-REM sleep rather than REM.16 Some popular articles credit REM with the brain’s clean-up. The stronger evidence so far points to deep sleep, and almost all of it comes from mice.

Hormones and the brain’s rinse cycle belong mostly to deep sleep. REM’s jobs look more like memory and mood.

What happens if you cut your sleep short

Because most REM sits in the second half of the night, an early alarm trims REM more than deep sleep. On a night when you are short of sleep, total REM can be cut short, and the brain may make up for it with more REM the next night. Sleep researchers call this REM rebound.259

4,000+Adults in two long-term studies, followed for 12 to 21 years.17
5% → 13%Each 5% less REM went with about a 13% higher death rate in the larger group.17
A linkNot proof. The authors say low REM may be a marker of poor health rather than its cause.17

A leading hypothesis also ties disrupted REM to mood problems, through its role in processing emotional memories.12 If you regularly sleep short or wake unrested, that is worth raising with a doctor.

Dreams, and the gamma question

REM is when most vivid, story-like dreams happen. Areas tied to memory (the hippocampus) and emotion (the amygdala) are active while you dream. It is not the only stage with dreams, though: dreaming can happen in any stage of sleep.110

Are gamma waves part of REM?

Yes. One review describes two modes the brain runs in. A slow mode in deep sleep, with delta waves. And a fast mode, in active waking and in REM sleep, with theta rhythm in the hippocampus and gamma rhythm across the rest of the cortex.6 So gamma is not missing from sleep. It shows up in the stage where you dream.

Is gamma the subconscious?

No brainwave band is the subconscious. Gamma is linked with active processing such as attention, perception and memory, and it appears both when you are alert and when you dream.116 “Subconscious” is not something an EEG measures.

40 HzA weak current at this rate, applied to the front and sides of the head during REM, raised reports of lucid dreaming.7
25 HzAlso raised it, less strongly. Sham stimulation produced no lucid dreams.7
Not soundThis was electrical stimulation in a sleep lab. A 40 Hz track has not been shown to do this.
Gamma in REM is not the same as gamma awake

In a 2016 study in cats, gamma coherence, a measure of how closely distant parts of the cortex move together at around 40 Hz, was highest during alert waking and almost absent during REM sleep. Gamma can be present in REM without the cortex being in sync the way it is when awake.8

Curious about the band itself? Gamma waves explained. The 40 Hz focus session is for daytime focus, and it is not a dreaming tool.

“I never dream”: what research says

Some people are sure they do not dream. The evidence says most of them dream and do not remember. Recall varies a lot from person to person. Dreaming itself looks universal.19

  • 289Patients studied in 2015, all with a disorder that makes people act out REM sleep instead of lying still.19
  • 8Of them said they had not recalled a dream for at least 10 years. Four said never, in their whole life.19
  • On videoDuring REM they argued, fought and spoke, scenes that look like dreams being acted out, then woke with no memory of them.19

One caution: everyone in that study had a sleep disorder, so it is strong evidence, not the final word for everyone.

Can you tell on your own if you got enough REM?

Not precisely. Telling REM from other stages takes a reading of brain activity, which only a sleep study (polysomnography) records. Watches and rings estimate stages from movement and heart rate.

Sleep labMeasures REM directly, with electrodes on the head.18
TrackerGood at sleep versus wake. In a 2021 lab test of seven devices, stage readings were inconsistent.18
YouBetter questions: enough hours in bed, at steady times, and do you feel rested during the day?

One everyday clue: waking up in the middle of a vivid dream usually means you were just in REM.5 If a tracker’s REM number worries you, how you feel during the day is the better guide. If you feel unrested for weeks, talk to a doctor.

REM vs deep sleep (delta)

The two stages are close to opposites. Deep sleep is slow and still. REM is fast and restless.

How REM changes with age

Newborns are the REM champions. They fall asleep straight into REM-like active sleep, not into non-REM, and the Sleep Foundation puts a newborn’s REM at around eight hours a day. By about three months, sleep starts with non-REM and REM shifts to the later part of the cycle.15

For adults, the Sleep Foundation’s rough figure is about two hours of REM a night. It changes from night to night.5

Can you get more REM sleep?

There is no proven trick that adds REM on demand. Because REM is packed into the second half of the night, the most reliable way not to lose it is simply to sleep long enough, at regular times. The Sleep Foundation also lists stress and alcohol or cannabis at night as things that can throw REM off. If a medicine might be changing your sleep, ask the person who prescribed it.25

REM sleep FAQ

What is REM sleep?

REM, short for rapid eye movement, is the stage of sleep when the eyes dart under closed lids, brain activity looks close to waking, most vivid dreams happen and most of the body's muscles go limp. In adults it is about 20 to 25 percent of the night.

How many times a night do you have REM sleep?

Once per sleep cycle. A night usually has four to six cycles of about 80 to 110 minutes, so most adults have four to six REM periods.

How long does REM sleep last?

The first REM period comes about 60 to 90 minutes after you fall asleep and is short, often around 10 minutes. Each one gets longer, and the last can run up to about an hour. Most REM sleep falls in the second half of the night.

Why can't you move during REM sleep?

During REM most skeletal muscles lose their tone, a state called atonia. The eyes and the diaphragm keep working. Researchers think this stops people from acting out their dreams.

Do you only dream in REM sleep?

No. Most vivid dreams happen in REM, but dreams can happen in any stage of sleep.

Why is REM sleep important?

Its exact purpose is still not settled. The strongest leads are memory, with REM helping to consolidate some kinds of learning, and emotion, with a leading hypothesis that REM takes the sting out of emotional memories. Researchers also think it supports brain development, which may be why newborns have so much of it.

Are hormones released during REM sleep?

REM is not the main hormone stage. Growth hormone has its biggest pulse shortly after you fall asleep, in deep slow-wave sleep. Cortisol rises in the later part of the night, mostly on the body clock. In the REM-rich second half of the night, growth hormone is no longer being released.

Does the brain clean itself during REM sleep?

The best evidence points to deep sleep, not REM. In mice, the brain cleared waste about twice as fast asleep as awake, and the flow of cleaning fluid tracked slow delta waves. Most of this work is in mice, and how it works in people is still being studied.

What happens if you get less REM sleep?

A short night trims REM, because most REM comes late. The brain may make up some of it the next night. In two long-term studies of over 4,000 adults, less REM was linked with a higher death rate, but that is a link, not proof that low REM causes harm.

Can a sleep tracker tell how much REM sleep I get?

Not reliably. Only a sleep study that records brain activity measures REM. In a 2021 test against lab recordings, consumer trackers were good at telling sleep from wake, but their sleep-stage readings were inconsistent.

Do people who never dream really not dream?

Probably they dream but do not remember. In a 2015 study of people with a disorder that makes them act out REM sleep, some who said they had not recalled a dream for years were filmed arguing, fighting and talking in REM, as if dreaming.

Are there gamma waves in REM sleep?

Yes. In REM the brain switches to a fast mode, with theta rhythm in the hippocampus and gamma in the cortex. In a 2014 lab study, a weak 40 Hz current applied during REM raised reports of lucid dreaming. That was electrical stimulation, not sound.
