How it works

Two tones in, one beat your brain makes

Each ear gets its own steady tone. Neither ear hears 10 Hz — the beat only exists once your brain puts the two together.

How to use it

Three steps, eleven minutes

  1. 1 Put on stereo headphones

    Each ear has to hear a different tone. Speakers blend the two channels together, so the beat never forms.

  2. 2 Press play

    The tone is generated live in your browser. Nothing to download, nothing to sign up for.

  3. 3 Stay with it for 11 minutes

    That is the window in which the synchronization settles. You can stop at any point.

Questions people ask

Before you press play

What is a binaural beat?
Two slightly different tones — one per ear. Your brain detects the difference and produces a third tone that doesn't physically exist. An auditory illusion with measurable neurological effects, first documented by physicist Heinrich Wilhelm Dove in 1839.

Why 10 Hz?
10Hz sits inside the Alpha band (8–13Hz) — the brain's natural state of relaxed alertness. Alpha is associated with reduced anxiety, creative clarity, and the quiet between stimulus and reaction. Present, not reactive.

What happens in the brain?
The brain synchronizes to the frequency it perceives — a process called neural entrainment. As you listen, your dominant brainwave frequency shifts toward 10Hz. Cortical noise decreases. Attention sharpens without effort.

Can it help with anxiety?
Alpha-band entrainment is associated with reduced anxiety and a calmer nervous system — the quiet between stimulus and reaction. ECHO.11 was born from exactly that need: a free tool for acute anxious moments, insomnia, and racing thoughts, available 24/7. It is a self-care practice, not a medical treatment — if anxiety is disrupting your life, please also talk to a professional.

What should I feel?
Individual response varies. Most commonly reported:
Mental clarity Reduced mind-chatter Gentle warmth Heightened focus Present-moment awareness
What the research actually shows

Not a cure.
A dimmer switch.

  • Small studies · modest effects

    The clearest effect is the nervous system, not memory or focus.

    Across binaural-beat research, the clearest, most consistent effect isn't cognitive — it's the nervous system. Several studies report lower self-rated anxiety, and some report lower pain ratings — effects that show up more consistently than any effect on memory or focus. The studies are small and the effects are modest. It doesn't numb sensation or heal tissue. It turns down the volume on your body's alarm system — the same pathway a slow exhale works through, just held there longer and more consistently than a breath alone can manage.

  • History · not evidence

    Research inspiration · Declassified 1983, released 2003

    In 1983, the US Army commissioned a classified analysis of The Monroe Institute's research into binaural frequencies and altered consciousness. The document — Analysis and Assessment of Gateway Process — was declassified and released by the CIA in 2003.

    It describes binaural beats as a way to shift brainwave activity and quiet cortical noise. This experience is inspired by that document — read it as a piece of history and a starting point for curiosity, not as evidence. The peer-reviewed research above is the part to weigh.

The 11-minute threshold

Research on neural entrainment suggests that 7–15 minutes is the window in which brainwave synchronization stabilizes. We chose 11 minutes because it sits inside that window with room to settle — long enough to stop checking the clock, short enough to actually do. It is a design choice, not a clinical threshold.

Safety

ECHO.11 is a self-care tool, not a medical device, and nothing here is medical advice. Do not listen while driving. If you have epilepsy or a seizure disorder, or you are fitted with a pacemaker or a cochlear implant, check with your doctor before using audio entrainment of any kind. This is not a crisis service: if you are in danger or thinking about harming yourself, contact your local emergency number or a crisis line right away.

The 10Hz session is not a diagnosis, a treatment, or a substitute for care from a doctor, therapist, or psychiatrist. If anxiety, pain, or sleeplessness is disrupting your life, please talk to someone qualified — this can sit alongside that work, but it does not replace it.

Response varies from person to person, and some people notice nothing at all. That is a normal outcome, not a failure on your part.

A few practical limits. Do not listen while driving or operating anything that needs your attention — the session is built for stillness with your eyes closed. Keep the volume low; the effect comes from the frequency, not the loudness. If a session leaves you dizzy, disoriented, or more agitated than when you started, stop and take the headphones off.
