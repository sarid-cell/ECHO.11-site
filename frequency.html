What is a binaural beat?
Two slightly different tones — one per ear. Your brain detects the difference and produces a third tone that doesn't physically exist. An auditory illusion with measurable neurological effects, first documented by physicist Heinrich Wilhelm Dove in 1839.
Why 10 Hz?
10Hz sits inside the Alpha band (8–13Hz) — the brain's natural state of relaxed alertness. Alpha is associated with reduced anxiety, creative clarity, and the quiet between stimulus and reaction. Present, not reactive.
What happens in the brain?
The brain synchronizes to the frequency it perceives — a process called neural entrainment. As you listen, your dominant brainwave frequency shifts toward 10Hz. Cortical noise decreases. Attention sharpens without effort.
Can it help with anxiety?
Alpha-band entrainment is associated with reduced anxiety and a calmer nervous system — the quiet between stimulus and reaction. ECHO.11 was born from exactly that need: a free tool for acute anxious moments, insomnia, and racing thoughts, available 24/7. It is a self-care practice, not a medical treatment — if anxiety is disrupting your life, please also talk to a professional.

Research Inspiration · Declassified 1983, Released 2003

In 1983, the US Army commissioned a classified analysis of The Monroe Institute's research into binaural frequencies and altered consciousness. The document — Analysis and Assessment of Gateway Process — was declassified and released by the CIA in 2003.

It concludes that binaural beat technology can alter consciousness in measurable, reproducible ways — reducing cortical noise, shifting brainwave activity, and creating conditions for expanded awareness. This experience is inspired by that document. It is a historical source, not a clinical trial.

What the research actually shows

Not a cure. A dimmer switch.

Across binaural-beat research, the clearest, most consistent effect isn't cognitive — it's the nervous system. Studies show measurable drops in anxiety and in how intensely pain registers, more reliably than any effect on memory or focus. It doesn't numb sensation or heal tissue. It turns down the volume on your body's alarm system — the same pathway a slow exhale works through, just held there longer and more consistently than a breath alone can manage.

Research findings, not medical claims. If you're managing chronic pain or an anxiety condition, this is a complement to care — not a replacement. Read the full disclaimer.
The 11-minute threshold
Research on neural entrainment suggests that 7–15 minutes is the window in which brainwave synchronization stabilizes. 11 minutes is the minimum effective session length for measurable Alpha entrainment — and the number at the core of ECHO.11's design philosophy.
The full disclaimer

ECHO.11 is a self-care tool, not a medical device, and nothing here is medical advice. The 10Hz session is not a diagnosis, a treatment, or a substitute for care from a doctor, therapist, or psychiatrist. If anxiety, pain, or sleeplessness is disrupting your life, please talk to someone qualified — this can sit alongside that work, but it does not replace it.

Response varies from person to person, and some people notice nothing at all. That is a normal outcome, not a failure on your part.

A few practical limits. Do not listen while driving or operating anything that needs your attention — the session is built for stillness with your eyes closed. If you have epilepsy or a seizure disorder, or you are fitted with a pacemaker or a cochlear implant, check with your doctor before using audio entrainment of any kind. Keep the volume low; the effect comes from the frequency, not the loudness. If a session leaves you dizzy, disoriented, or more agitated than when you started, stop and take the headphones off.

The research described on this page is drawn from published work on binaural beats and neural entrainment. It reports what studies have observed in general — not what will happen to you, and no efficacy figures are claimed for this specific recording.

This is not a crisis service. If you are in danger or thinking about harming yourself, contact your local emergency number or a crisis line right away.
