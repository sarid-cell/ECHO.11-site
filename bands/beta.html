What Are Beta Waves?

Beta waves are brain rhythms of roughly 13 to 30 cycles per second (13–30 Hz), and the most common rhythm in the EEG of an awake adult. They are small, fast and fairly irregular, and clearest over the front and center of the head. Over the motor areas, beta drops when you move and rebounds when you stop.12

Watch beta drop when you move

Beta is easiest to understand through your hands. Over the motor areas of the brain, it falls while a movement is planned and made, then jumps above its starting level for a moment once the movement ends. Studies in humans and monkeys agree on this pattern.23

The drop shows up even when people only imagine a movement or watch someone else make one, so it follows the brain’s motor planning, not just the muscles.4

What the band is

Beta covers roughly 13 to 30 Hz. It is what an awake adult’s brain produces most of the time, so you are making it while you read this.1 Researchers often split it into low and high beta, which may do different jobs, but the cut-offs vary between labs.4 Below beta sit the calmer alpha waves; above it sit the faster gamma waves.

Beta is not a state you reach. It is the rhythm of simply being awake.

What beta might be for: keeping things steady

One influential idea, from Andreas Engel and Pascal Fries in 2010, is that beta signals the status quo. It is stronger when the brain intends to keep its current motor or mental state, and weaker when a change is expected.5 That fits the movement pattern: beta falls just as you are about to change what your body is doing.

They also proposed that abnormally strong beta, as seen in some movement disorders, could hold the brain in its current state and make flexible behavior harder.5 It is a hypothesis, not a settled answer: a 2021 review lists many competing proposals for what sensorimotor beta does.4

Does anxiety mean too much beta?

You will often read that anxiety is a “high beta” state. The research is far less tidy. A 2019 review of 184 resting EEG studies across ten mental health conditions, anxiety among them, found that changes in the frequency bands overlap a lot between conditions, are usually small, and track symptom severity only weakly.6

There is one more twist. Sedative medicines such as benzodiazepines increase beta on an EEG.1 So more beta cannot simply mean a more anxious brain.

Can sound boost your beta waves?

Beta is the band most often sold as “focus” or “productivity” audio. The evidence does not back that up.

In a 2017 study, a beta-rate beat of about 18 Hz played for three minutes produced no change in EEG beta power, and no change in heart rate or skin conductance.8 A 2023 systematic review of 14 EEG studies found that brainwaves followed the beat in only five of them.7 A 2023 meta-analysis on memory and attention did find a moderate overall effect for binaural beats, but conflicting results for beta beats in particular.9

Being awake is not something a tone has to supply. You already have it.

What beta waves are not

Beta is not a focus dial, and more of it is not better. It is the background rhythm of being awake. Framing it as a state to reach is like selling someone their own pulse.

ECHO.11 has no beta session, and that is deliberate. Building one would mean claiming an effect the evidence does not support.

Beta waves FAQ

What frequency range is the beta band?

The EEG beta band is usually defined as 13 to 30 Hz, meaning 13 to 30 cycles per second. Researchers often split it into low and high beta, which may do different jobs, but the exact cut-offs vary between labs.14

What are the characteristics of beta waves?

Compared with alpha, beta waves are faster, smaller and less regular. In adults they are usually about 10 to 20 microvolts, strongest over the front and center of the head, and the most common rhythm in a normal waking EEG.1

What is the difference between alpha and beta waves?

Alpha (8 to 13 Hz) is the rhythm of resting awake with the eyes closed, strongest over the back of the head. Beta (13 to 30 Hz) is faster and smaller, strongest over the front and center of the head, and is the most common rhythm in a normal waking EEG.1

Why do beta waves drop when you move?

Over the motor areas of the brain, beta power falls just before and during a movement, then briefly rises above its starting level once the movement stops. This is called the beta rebound. The drop also appears when people only imagine or watch a movement.234

Do beta binaural beats improve focus?

There is no clear evidence that they do. A 2017 study played a beta-rate beat of about 18 Hz and found no change in EEG beta power, and a 2023 meta-analysis on memory and attention found conflicting results for beta beats in particular.89

Does ECHO.11 have a beta session?

No, and that is deliberate. The sessions on the site are 10 Hz in the alpha range, 40 Hz in the gamma range, and a sleep wind-down. They are listening sessions, not brainwave training.
