What Are Alpha Waves?

Alpha waves are brain rhythms of about 8 to 13 cycles per second (8–13 Hz). On an EEG they are easiest to see when you are awake, relaxed and have your eyes closed. They are strongest over the back of the head and shrink as soon as the eyes open. Hans Berger recorded them in the first published human EEG, in 1929.12

See alpha appear and vanish

The single most reliable thing about alpha is how it reacts to your eyelids. Close them and a big, even wave grows over the back of the head. Open them and it breaks apart. Try it below.123

What the band is

Alpha runs roughly 8 to 13 Hz and is the best-known EEG band, because it is the easiest one to see. Clinicians call it the posterior dominant rhythm: in a relaxed adult with closed eyes, it is the main rhythm over the back of the head.2 Below alpha sit theta and the slower delta waves of deep sleep. Above it sit the faster beta waves of alert thinking.

Ten hertz is a handy round number, but it is an average, not a fixed setting. In brain recordings from 51 adults, the average alpha peak was about 10.3 Hz. It differed between people, and it moved up within the same person as a memory task got harder.4

Alpha is the easiest brain rhythm to see: close your eyes and it grows, open them and it shrinks.

Idle or gatekeeper? Two ideas about alpha

For decades, alpha was read as the signal of a brain area doing nothing. A newer view says close to the opposite: alpha is the brain actively holding a door shut. Which one is right changes what a rise in alpha means.

The gatekeeper view now has strong support, and its authors argue that the evidence has turned against pure idling.7 Both views agree on one thing: alpha alone does not tell you how calm someone feels.

What alpha waves are linked to

Arousal, on average. In a 2007 study of 28 students, alpha fell across the scalp when they opened their eyes, while skin conductance, a standard measure of arousal, rose. With eyes closed, people with lower arousal showed more alpha.3

Attention. Alpha drops over the parts of the brain you are using and rises over the parts you are tuning out.67

Anxiety, in binaural-beat studies. In the meta-analysis by Garcia-Argibay, Santed and Reales (2019, Psychological Research), the effect binaural beats showed most consistently was a reduction in anxiety, with a much less consistent picture for memory and attention.8 That is why ECHO.11's alpha session is framed around calm rather than focus. Note that this finding is about how anxious listeners said they felt, not about measured alpha waves.

Can sound change your alpha waves?

This is the question behind every “alpha waves music” video, so it deserves a straight answer: not reliably, as far as the evidence goes.

A 2023 systematic review collected 14 EEG studies of binaural beats. Five reported brainwaves following the beat, eight did not, and one was mixed. The studies used very different methods, which makes them hard to compare.9

One cleaner test played beats at five rates, including an alpha-rate beat of about 9 Hz, for three minutes each. Alpha power did not change, and neither did heart rate or skin conductance.10

If a session helps you, part of the reason may be eleven quiet minutes with your eyes closed, which is exactly when alpha shows up on its own.

What alpha waves are not

Alpha is not a measure of how relaxed you are. It tracks arousal on average, across groups of people,3 and even its peak speed differs from one person to the next.4 An audio session that does nothing visible for you is not a failure on your part.

It is also not a treatment for an anxiety disorder. The research describes group-level changes in anxiety scores, not a clinical effect on a diagnosis. If anxiety is disrupting your life, that belongs with a professional; a session can sit alongside that work, not in place of it.

Alpha waves FAQ

What are alpha waves on an EEG?

Alpha waves are a rhythm of about 8 to 13 Hz recorded from the scalp. On an EEG they look like a regular, rounded wave over the back of the head that appears when the eyes close, which is why clinicians call alpha the posterior dominant rhythm in adults.2

What frequency is the alpha band?

Most references define the EEG alpha band as 8 to 13 Hz, and many studies use 8 to 12 Hz. In brain recordings from 51 adults, the average alpha peak was about 10.3 Hz, and it shifted from person to person and rose as tasks got harder.24

Why do alpha waves drop when you open your eyes?

When the eyes open and the visual system starts taking in the world, the large, synchronized alpha rhythm breaks up into smaller, faster activity. This is called alpha blocking, and it is the effect Hans Berger described in 1929.12

Are alpha waves a sign of relaxation?

Loosely, and on average. In a 2007 study, people with lower arousal showed more alpha with their eyes closed, and alpha fell when they opened their eyes. But alpha is not a calm-meter: it also rises over brain areas that a task needs to tune out.37

Can binaural beats increase alpha waves?

The evidence is mixed. A 2023 systematic review of 14 EEG studies found 5 that supported the idea that brainwaves follow the beat, 8 that did not, and 1 with mixed results. A 2017 study that played an alpha-rate beat of about 9 Hz for three minutes found no change in alpha power.910
