What Are Alpha Waves?

By Shira Sarid · Updated October 16, 2026

8–13 Hz Calm Session: 10 Hz · The 11-Minute Reset

Alpha waves are brainwaves of roughly 8 to 13 cycles per second (Hz) — the rhythm that rises on an EEG when a relaxed person closes their eyes and drops again when they open them. They are strongest over the back of the head, where the visual cortex is, and they were the first rhythm described in a human EEG, by Hans Berger in 1929[1].

What the band is

Alpha runs roughly 8 to 13 Hz and is the best known of the EEG bands, because it is the one that is easiest to see. It rises when a relaxed person closes their eyes and drops again when they open them — a change clear enough that Hans Berger described it in the first published human EEG recording (Berger, 1929, Archiv für Psychiatrie und Nervenkrankheiten)[1]. It is strongest over the back of the head, where the visual cortex is. Below alpha sit theta and the slower delta waves of deep sleep.

What alpha waves are linked to

The meta-analysis by Garcia-Argibay, Santed and Reales (2019, Psychological Research)[2] found the most consistent effect of binaural beats across studies was a reduction in anxiety, with weaker and less consistent findings for memory and attention. That is the clearest reason ECHO.11's alpha session is framed around calm rather than around focus.

Alpha is often described as an idling rhythm, but it has also been argued to reflect active inhibition — the brain suppressing input it does not need rather than doing nothing. Which description is right changes what a rise in alpha would mean.

Alpha-band responses to rhythmic auditory stimulation have been measured, but the size and durability of any shift, and whether it outlasts the sound, are not settled.

What alpha waves are not

Alpha is not a measure of how relaxed you are. Two people can feel equally calm and produce very different amounts of it, and some people produce very little alpha at all under any condition. An audio session that does nothing visible for you is not a failure on your part.

It is also not a treatment for an anxiety disorder. The research describes group-level changes in anxiety scores, not a clinical effect on a diagnosis. If anxiety is disrupting your life, that belongs with a professional; a session can sit alongside that work, not in place of it.

What listeners report

Individual response varies, and some people notice nothing. Most commonly reported:

Mental clarity Reduced mind-chatter Gentle warmth Present-moment awareness

Alpha waves FAQ

What are alpha waves?

Alpha runs roughly 8 to 13 Hz and rises when a relaxed person closes their eyes. Hans Berger described it in the first published human EEG in 1929.

What frequency are alpha waves?

Alpha waves run roughly 8 to 13 Hz, meaning 8 to 13 cycles per second. That places them between theta (4–8 Hz) and beta (13–30 Hz).

Do more alpha waves mean you are more relaxed?

Not reliably. Two people can feel equally calm and produce very different amounts of alpha, and some people produce very little alpha under any condition.

Do alpha binaural beats reduce anxiety?

The meta-analysis by Garcia-Argibay, Santed and Reales (2019) found reduced anxiety was the most consistent effect of binaural beats across studies. That is a group-level change in anxiety scores, not a treatment for an anxiety disorder.
